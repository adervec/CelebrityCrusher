The Celebrity Endurance Ledger

Part one: verified marathon personal bests, age on race day, and what each life actually looked like at the start line — with one projection pinned where it lands: Georgina, 13 September 2026. Part two: everything beyond 42.195 — channels, summits, poles, mats, one breath held for seven minutes, and a historical wing going back to 1726.

The reference point for the entire genre is still 4:29:20 — the Oprah line, Marine Corps 1994. Everything above and below it is measured against a talk-show host who trained in secret at 4:30 a.m.

The Canadian Line

Terry Fox, 1980: 5,373 km in 143 days — a marathon a day, every day, on one leg, at 21, until the cancer returned. No table holds that row. It goes here, apart, where it belongs.

† Pro asterisk Armstrong is included for scale only — a career endurance professional (with the record that entails) is not the same species as the rest of the marathon table. Retired pro athletes are likewise excluded from the feats table; Kona is littered with ex-NFL and ex-NHL bodies.

* Approximations Times marked with an asterisk (Schulman, Farrell, Diplo, Flay, Lopez, Aduba, Huckabee) circulate without seconds in press accounts; Murakami's ≈3:27 is self-reported in his memoir; Beatrice's caterpillar finish is reported to the minute at best. Farrell's fully timed Dublin 2024 finish was 4:06:45.

The Morissette problem Alanis Morissette's marathon best is reported variously as 3:34, 4:17, and 4:28 depending on outlet and race — unresolvable enough to keep Canada's most decorated triathlete-musician off the table entirely.

Goalkeeper corner Albert Camus (Racing Universitaire d'Alger), Vladimir Nabokov (Trinity College, Cambridge), the young Karol Wojtyła — later John Paul II — and a teenage Julio Iglesias (Real Madrid youth, until a car crash rerouted him to the guitar) all kept goal before their other careers. Literature and the papacy apparently select for the position.

Belt-rank corner Guy Ritchie and Ed O'Neill both hold legitimate Brazilian jiu-jitsu black belts. Rank, not race — so no row.

Sources & variance Times and figures as reported in race results and contemporaneous press.
